Safety

Updated: November 10, 2026

Review while parked

Set up the app, start or stop drives manually, and review reports only while safely parked. Optional CarPlay automations are for people who already have and use CarPlay; they are not required to record trips.

Posted limits take priority

Speed limits come from community-maintained OpenStreetMap data. They can be missing, outdated or wrong, especially on smaller neighborhood roads. Matching can select the wrong road, and temporary, conditional or variable limits may not be represented. Follow posted signs and actual road conditions. Drive Probe is a retrospective review tool, not a navigation, emergency or enforcement service.

Saved drives may be rechecked using newer downloaded road data. Rechecked reviews identify the map dates used; those data may differ from posted limits when the drive was recorded.

Events are estimates

GPS, phone placement, road conditions and iOS background restrictions can affect recording and event detection. Possible acceleration, braking and speeding events need human review. An unlock-related event is an experimental signal, not proof of phone use or who was driving. The app cannot identify every interaction with another app or distinguish all permitted CarPlay interactions.

Improve missing data

Anyone can contribute local knowledge to OpenStreetMap. The missing-speed-limit report in Offline maps helps identify roads worth checking. Verify limits using permitted sources such as posted signs; do not copy from proprietary maps or databases. Contributions become available in Drive Probe only after a newer source snapshot is built into a map package and that update is downloaded. Updating OpenStreetMap does not immediately update your phone.
